# cybersecurity-portfolio

## About Me: 
I remember the first time I heard, "You've Got Mail!". Emails are less exciting these days, but my enthusiasm for technology remains. As a kid I tinkered with computers whenever I could; usually my big brother's computer when he wasn't home. On more than one occasion, I infected his computer with malware. As angry as he was, he let me watch him fix it. Looking back, I suppose those were my first experiences with cybersecurity.
At 27, I decided to change careers. I wasn't sure exactly where it would lead, but I knew I wanted to give technology a real shot. I enrolled at the University of Michigan with plans to major in Information Systems Management, and I landed an early internship in IT Infrastructure Support. My boss encouraged me to take entry-level Cisco networking courses and then to pursue the GSEC through SANS. That SANS course was a turning point. I paused my studies at UofM, completed an undergraduate certificate with SANS Technology Institute, and transferred into the Bachelor of Applied Cybersecurity (BACS) program. I expect to graduate in December 2026.
That internship grew into an eight-year career with the same company. Two months ago I started at a new company as an IT Specialist, a position that offers a better work-life balance now that I'm a mom to a one-year-old. I love my new job. My work focuses on improving our security posture, managing identity and access (IAM), building reporting and logging for SOC 2 compliance, and supporting our users. I bring dedication, motivation, and genuine enthusiasm to all of it.

## My NCL CTF experience: 
This season I competed in the National Cyber League (NCL) Practice Game, Individual Game, and Team Game. All three were a real challenge. The games covered a wide range of material: some of it I work with regularly, and some I hadn't reviewed in a long time. Working through it pushed me to refresh older skills, sharpen the ones I use every day, and learn new techniques along the way.
Most of all, it was fun. The Practice and Individual Games showed me what I could do on my own and where I still had room to grow. The Team Game was the best part of the whole experience.

## NCL Highlight - Team Game Experience: 
My biggest highlight was the people I competed with in the Team Game. Over the course of the game we spent roughly 15 to 20 hours together on calls, talking through challenges, comparing approaches, and sharing our experiences. Everyone participated, everyone was enthusiastic, and everyone contributed. There were no slackers. It was the most solid team I've ever worked with.
That experience reinforced something I see in my day-to-day work as well: security is a team effort. When everyone brings their own strengths and stays engaged, the group can solve far more than any one person could alone. 
Going into the NCL games, I had a mild case of senioritis, along with new-mom burnout, sleep deprivation, and new-job jitters. Working alongside such a motivated team reenergized me and reminded me why I chose this path. I came out of the season with renewed drive to finish strong and continue building a meaningful career.
